### Notes per pedal

- **AnalogMan Sun Face BC183**: Silicon Fuzz Face, unbuffered: must be FIRST in the chain, guitar straight into it. Ships battery-only; check your unit has the optional DC jack. Silicon version is negative ground so sharing power is OK, but AnalogMan warns a supply can add hum/oscillation. 'Under 1 mA' is from the shared Fuzz Face page. Power re-checked 2026-10-21: no source publishes a current draw, so 1 mA is unverified; AnalogMan never forbids higher than 9V but gives no range either.
- **Danelectro 3699 Fuzz**: Fuzz/octaver, candidate to replace the JHS Double Dragon in the front fuzz group. 9 mA is from a single retailer listing (another says 3 mA; no Danelectro spec sheet found), so planning on 9 mA. Spec lists 9V DC, supply not included; a 9V battery is reported to work. Assumed 9V only. Planned for the gig board, between the Sun Face and the Beano. True bypass per retailer spec lists (no Danelectro spec sheet found).
- **AnalogMan Beano Boost**: Germanium treble booster, direct post-fuzz. Per AnalogMan it can share a DC supply with the Sun Face and other basic fuzzes (keep that group simple). The Oct 2020 manual text read by the research agent said isolated power only / no daisy-chaining, so confirm the wording for your unit. Never 9V AC. Needs the DC jack option (battery by default). Center-negative.
- **TC Electronic PolyTune 3 Mini**: The owner's unit is the PolyTune 3 MINI (Noir; owner, 2026-11-06). Tuner / master front buffer. 100 mA is the manual's minimum supply rating; measured draw is lower (~38 mA reported for the Mini). Bypass mode is selectable by DIP switch (true by default, buffered, or buffered with display on); buffer ENABLED in this plan as the master front buffer. Has a power-out jack for daisy-chaining. Power re-checked 2026-10-21: actual draw 45 mA (full size) / 40 mA (Mini), but TC asks for a >100 mA supply and one Noir listing says 100 mA, so 100 mA stays as the worst case.
- **Wampler Mini Ego Compressor**: Compressor. Wampler publishes 14 mA at 9V and 22 mA at 18V (figure here is the 18V one). Page does not explicitly state the jack is 18V-rated. True bypass. Listed as 'Ego Mini' in the original plan.
- **Fulltone Full-Drive 2 Mosfet**: Overdrive, more headroom at 18V. ~10 mA is from Fulltone's page for the newer v2; no manufacturer figure found for the original Mosfet model (one forum measurement: ~7 mA). 15 mA = the owner's worst case (2026-11-06).
- **JAM Pedals Retrovibe**: Photocell Uni-Vibe, pre-dirt for throbbing fuzz-vibe tones. The owner's unit is the Mk3 (owner, 2026-11-06): 150 mA = the worst case of the Mk3 listings (80-150 mA; the Mk4 is 120 mA). True bypass, no battery. Exempt from 'modulation after gain': intentionally ahead of the dirt.
- **MXR Phase 95**: Phaser, pre-dirt for a classic 70s sweep. No battery option. True bypass. Exempt from 'modulation after gain': intentionally ahead of the dirt.
- **JAM Pedals Red Muck Mk2**: Russian/Civil War Muff fuzz. Sources conflict: 60 mA (JAM shop page) vs 12-15 mA (retailers); planning on 60 mA until the manual is checked. True bypass. Power re-checked 2026-10-21: 12 mA on most listings, 15 mA on Thomann (planning on 15); the old 60 mA appears in no source.
- **Wampler Ratsbane**: Hard-clipping distortion. 2.5 mA at 9V, 3.2 mA at 18V; approved for 18V but designed around 9V. No battery connector. True bypass.
- **Browne Protein V3**: Dual overdrive, placed post-Ratsbane to stack or smooth the hard clipping. 9V only ('18V is not better'). Fully analog (per owner), so it may share an output. True bypass (per owner; V3 uses soft/electronic switching per Browne). This is the V3. Browne's sources conflict for V3 current: manual says <100 mA, a Browne Q&A line says 130 mA, retailers quote ~30 mA. Planning on 130 mA (worst case).
- **Wampler Tumnus Deluxe**: Klon-style EQ/overdrive: dead last, master mid-stack and buffer. Runs at 9V ONLY (boosts the rail internally, do not use 18V). Buffered or true bypass via side switch (buffer enabled in this plan). 70.3 mA at 9V. Tagged 'gain' (it is a drive) but 'dead last' outranks the group-order rules via priority.
- **MXR Analog Chorus**: Analog BBD chorus (M234). Buffered bypass per the Dunlop manual (one reviewer says it dulls highs when off). Battery or 9V DC.
- **PastFX Reticon Flanger**: Analog BBD flanger, post-dirt modulation. Needs an isolated 18V output, no battery. True bypass (per owner). 'Under 50 mA' is from search snippets of the PastFX page/manual (direct fetch failed): verify.
- **JAM Pedals Harmonious Monk Mk2**: Harmonic tremolo with digital tap controller. Analog signal path but a digital processor, so treated as DIGITAL: must not share an output. JAM recommends an isolated supply; 145 mA is JAM's max (third-party listings say 35-100 mA). True bypass.
- **Catalinbread Belle Epoch Deluxe**: Tape echo emulation. Analog EP-3-style circuitry around a 24-bit digital delay line, so treated as DIGITAL. Catalinbread lists 9V DC ONLY at 200 mA (it steps up to an internal rail itself): the original plan had it on 18V, which could damage it, so it is on a 9V output here. Give it a clean isolated output. Spelled 'Epoque' in the original plan.
- **BOSS DD-200 Digital Delay**: In guitar-gig (replaced the DD-8, owner layout 2026-11-04). Digital delay with 2 footswitches, memories, MIDI, 60 s looper. BOSS spec page (boss.info/global/products/dd-200/specifications): current draw 225 mA (high), power = 3 AA batteries or PSA-S series AC adaptor (sold separately), bypass 'Buffered bypass' ('Analog dry through and buffered bypass'). Center-negative: confirmed by the owner (2026-11-05; also a UK retailer listing and the PSA-S adaptor BOSS specifies, Roland support). The BOSS page gives no DC voltage; 9 V DC is from Thomann (unverified against the manual). Digital: must not share an output; 225 mA needs an output rated for it.

## Cable routing

Signal cables are routed on the board surface between the pedals: never in front of the lowest pedals, at most 3.5 mm beyond the board edges at the back and sides (the usable board area, the dashed teal line of `board.json` `bounds`, limits the pedals only, not the cables), at most 2 cables stacked in one 2 mm cell (cable width on the board 3.5 mm). A cable may also pass under the board when that is shorter or less crowded: it goes down in a hole cell (a grid cell whose centre lies in a hole that no pedal covers), or round the board edge along an edge segment the owner drew in the board editor (`board.json`, type `edge`), runs under the board (only the under-board supplies are obstacles there, at most 2 cables stacked) and comes up in a hole or round an edge segment. A pass through the deck adds 15 mm of cable and a crossing of the board edge 30 mm (twice the hole transit), each costs 15 mm more in the search, a mm under the board counts 0.9 in the search (these numbers are unverified estimates; `cables.under_board: false` keeps every cable on top). Plugs are modelled (default cable type `ernie_ball_flat_patch_ra`, catalog/cables.json): a right-angle plug's cable leaves the head to the left or right of the jack's facing (chosen per end: fewest blocked plugs and plug collisions, then the shortest route), runs 3.5 mm straight, then is routed. A right-angle plug may also point straight down into a board hole (`down` in the Exits column; the square of one cable width at the plug's bend point must lie inside the holes and clear of the under-board supplies): where that is possible it is always chosen, even if the cable gets longer, and the cable continues under the board. The cable down to the hole is the jack height (`cables.jack_height_mm`, 12 mm, an unverified estimate of the jack axis above the deck) plus the deck (15 mm). `cables.overrides.<id>.exit_a` / `exit_b` accept `down`. Power cables are routed with the same router on their own grid (3.2 mm wide; plug `dc_ra_owner_baseline`, exit side in the Exits column) and count against each other only: at most 2 stacked per cell and layer, at most 2 across a power plug. They stay under the board as much as they can: a cable from an under-board supply port starts under the board and comes up through the usable hole nearest its pedal's power jack (a hole cell no pedal covers) or round the board edge along an edge segment the owner drew in the board editor (`board.json`, type `edge`; none drawn: holes only; with neither holes nor edge segments an under-board supply is an error), then runs the short way on top to the jack; a hop from a pedal's power jack (daisy chain) or from an on-board supply starts on top. In the search a mm on top counts 3 (under the board 1). A pass through a hole adds 15 mm of cable and a crossing of the board edge 30 mm (both unverified estimates; the edge value is twice the hole transit). A power cable that runs more than 150 mm on top is reported.

### DC cables to buy

A **serial** output is one cable that runs from the supply port to the first pedal and on to the next ones (a plug at the port and one at each pedal); its legs P#.1, P#.2... ## Footswitches

Footswitch data: 17 of 17 pedals (catalog/footswitches.json).

Spacing is comfort, not a hard rule: switches of different pedals should be as far apart as the other goals allow. Crowding of a pair = priority x (comfort - distance)^2 / comfort for pairs closer than the comfort distance (100 mm, `footswitches.comfort_mm`; distance centre to centre; priority per switch type, `footswitches.priority`); lower is better, the layout proposals minimise it. No minimum distance is set. Switches on the same pedal are not checked. Type: bypass (turns the pedal, or one side of it, on and off), tap (tap tempo), boost (a boost / extra-gain stomp), function (any other switch).

Toppers (a larger, higher cap on a switch) are a finishing step after the layout; the layout search ignores them. A topper adds 4.5 mm (`footswitches.topper_bonus_mm`; default: the height a topper adds, a rough proxy, unverified) to the effective distance of every pair its switch is in (both switches of a pair: twice); crowding uses the effective distance. No toppers are set.

Chosen after the layout (never during the search), on bypass switches only (a tap switch gets one by hand, a boost switch never), greedily: the topper that removes the most crowding first (a switch in several close pairs helps them all; ties: the switch in more crowded pairs, then the higher priority type, then nearer the front), at most 5 in all (`footswitches.max_toppers`), stopping when a topper would remove less than 2 % of the crowding (`footswitches.topper_min_gain`).
